# Strait of Hormuz: key data

The Strait of Hormuz is a narrow waterway between Iran and Oman connecting the Persian Gulf with the Gulf of Oman and Arabian Sea.

| Indicator | Data |
|---|---:|
| Width at narrowest point | Approximately **33–34 km (21 miles)** |
| Navigation lanes | Roughly **2 miles wide in each direction**, separated by a buffer zone |
| Oil transported in 2025 | **19.87 million barrels/day** |
| Crude and condensate | **14.95 million barrels/day** |
| Refined petroleum products | **4.93 million barrels/day** |
| Share of global seaborne oil trade | Around **25%** |
| Share destined for Asia | About **80%** |
| LNG transported in 2025 | Just over **112 billion cubic metres** |
| Share of global LNG trade | Approximately **19–20%** |
| Estimated alternative pipeline capacity | Only **3.5–5.5 million barrels/day** |

The leading 2025 oil shippers through the strait were Saudi Arabia—**6.23 million barrels/day**—Iraq (**3.63 million**), the UAE (**3.24 million**), Iran (**2.41 million**) and Kuwait (**2.37 million**). [International Energy Agency](https://www.iea.org/about/oil-security-and-emergency-ission/strait-of-hormuz)

## Current situation

Shipping and energy flows are severely disrupted as of September 2026:

- Oil movements through Hormuz averaged about **4.9 million barrels/day in Q2 2026**, versus **21.6 million barrels/day in Q4 2025**. [U.S. EIA September 2026 outlook](https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/pdf/steo_text.pdf)
- Estimated regional crude-production shutdowns reached **6.7 million barrels/day in August 2026**.
- AIS-derived data indicate an average of approximately **4.2 commercial ship transits per day** during the 30 days ending 13 September, about **95% below** the pre-conflict average. These counts likely understate traffic because some vessels switch off or manipulate their transponders. [IEA Shipping Monitor](https://www.iea.org/data-and-statistics/data-tools/middle-east-maritime-chokepoints-shipping-monitor)
- The IMO reported around **20,000 seafarers**, plus port and offshore workers, affected in the region. By 15 September, it had recorded **80 confirmed maritime incidents** connected with the wider crisis. [International Maritime Organization](https://www.imo.org/en/mediacentre/hottopics/pages/middle-east-strait-of-hormuz.aspx)

Its strategic importance comes from the combination of very large energy volumes and limited alternatives: Iran, Iraq, Kuwait, Qatar and Bahrain depend on Hormuz for most of their maritime energy exports, while only Saudi Arabia and the UAE possess substantial operational bypass pipelines.
